A flower can begin as a few stitches, become a scratched line, or return as a pattern on a pot. Across Artworks, ScrapingBy, and Embroideries, I keep finding different ways to explore familiar shapes.

In **Embroideries**, a line grows one stitch at a time. Thread adds colour and texture, while the fabric gives it something to respond to. A worn patch, an unfinished edge, or a small piece of saved cloth can suggest what comes next. Sometimes the work is decorative; sometimes it gives a useful object a little more life.

<Photo collection="embroideries" src="emilie_idea_embroideries_2026-05-06_DYAgPQTjc8a_01.jpg" alt="Hand-stitched unicorn and crescent moon patches on grey fabric" />

**ScrapingBy** involves a different kind of attention. Instead of adding thread, I remove parts of a surface. Small marks reveal light and detail, and the areas left untouched become part of the image. Flowers and leaves give me plenty to explore: overlapping petals, fine veins, shadows, and the spaces between them.

<Photo collection="scrapingby" src="eco_zhonglaise_2023-01-15_CncgbN7todm_01.jpg" alt="Botanical scratchboard with pale petals against a dark surface" />

In **Artworks**, those interests move through pottery, printmaking, and stitched pieces. Cutting a lino block makes me think about which marks will hold ink and which will remain clear. Working on pottery brings shape into the picture, as a design travels around a curved surface.

The galleries include work in progress because those stages matter to me too: the first marks, the tools on the table, the piece waiting for the kiln. They show how an idea changes as I work with it.
